The Traditionalist International: How Perennial Philosophy Became the Ideological Engine of Global Authoritarian Convergence
Executive Summary
This report argues that a coherent, transnational ideological framework underpins the apparent convergence of disparate global authoritarian movements. This framework is rooted in the esoteric philosophy of Traditionalism, particularly the works of René Guénon and Julius Evola. The analysis demonstrates how Traditionalist tenets—such as the rejection of modernity, the belief in a cyclical history culminating in a "dark age" (Kali Yuga), and the call for a return to a sacred, hierarchical order—have been adapted and weaponized by political actors from Washington to Moscow, Beijing, New Delhi, and Ankara.
Key figures like Steve Bannon and Leonard Leo in the U.S., Vladimir Putin and Aleksandr Dugin in Russia, Xi Jinping in China, Narendra Modi in India, and Recep Tayyip Erdoğan in Turkey, while operating in distinct cultural contexts, are shown to be nodes in an informal "Traditionalist International." This network leverages Perennial Philosophy to legitimize authoritarian governance, frame geopolitical conflict as a "clash of civilizations," and challenge the liberal international order. The report concludes that understanding this hidden ideological infrastructure is critical for comprehending the resilience and appeal of modern authoritarianism and for formulating an effective response from liberal democracies.
Introduction: The Hidden Ideological Infrastructure of a New World Order
The Limits of Conventional Analysis
The 21st century has witnessed a marked resurgence of authoritarianism, a trend often attributed to a confluence of familiar factors: economic inequality, the disruptive force of technology, a populist backlash against globalization, or the reassertion of nationalist sentiment. While these elements are undeniably significant, they fail to account for the striking ideological coherence that has emerged across a diverse array of illiberal and authoritarian movements. Leaders operating in vastly different cultural, religious, and political milieus have converged around a shared intellectual framework that provides a deeper, more resonant justification for their anti-democratic projects. This report posits that a common philosophical grammar is at work, one that transcends conventional political analysis and points to a hidden ideological infrastructure driving this global convergence.
Thesis: The Traditionalist International
This report introduces the concept of the "Traditionalist International," not as a formal, conspiratorial organization, but as a transnational intellectual and political ecosystem. This ecosystem is united by a common allegiance to the principles of Perennial Philosophy, particularly in its radical, anti-modern form known as Traditionalism. This philosophy functions as a flexible yet consistent ideological engine for anti-liberal political projects. It provides a metaphysical justification for rejecting the core tenets of the modern West—individualism, materialism, liberal democracy, and the idea of linear progress—and for restoring a hierarchical social order rooted in what it defines as sacred, timeless truth.
Mapping the Convergence
The analysis that follows will map this convergence by first dissecting the philosophical source code of Traditionalism and then examining its political applications in key global contexts. The report will explore the networks of Steve Bannon and Leonard Leo in the United States, Vladimir Putin's state ideology in Russia, Xi Jinping's use of tradition in China, Narendra Modi's Hindu nationalism in India, and Recep Tayyip Erdoğan's neo-Ottomanism in Turkey. Each case study will demonstrate how these distinct movements, while preserving their local cultural and religious identities, draw from the same Traditionalist wellspring to legitimize authoritarian political structures and wage a coordinated, if not centrally directed, assault on the liberal international order.
Part I: The Philosophical Bedrock – From Perennial Wisdom to Political Revolt
This section establishes the core philosophical concepts that form the foundation of the entire report. It will meticulously detail the ideas of Perennialism and its radical offshoot, Traditionalism, distinguishing between the metaphysical critique of René Guénon and the political-martial activism of Julius Evola.
The Perennial Philosophy: A Universalist Foundation
At the heart of the Traditionalist worldview lies the Perennial Philosophy, or philosophia perennis, a school of thought positing that a single, universal metaphysical truth is the common source of all orthodox world religions and mystical traditions. This philosophy asserts that recurring themes across global spiritualities illuminate universal truths about the nature of reality, humanity, and ethics. Its central tenet, expressed in the Hindu formula tat tvam asi ('That thou art'), is that the immanent, eternal Self (Atman) is one with the Absolute Principle of all existence (Brahman). The ultimate purpose of human life, therefore, is to realize this divine identity.
The idea originated in the Renaissance with Neo-Platonist thinkers like Marsilio Ficino and Agostino Steuco, who sought to synthesize diverse wisdom traditions—from Hermeticism to Christianity—into a single, timeless philosophy, or prisca theologia, that they believed was present in all ages.
It is crucial, however, to distinguish this general perennialism from the radical, anti-modern Traditionalism (with a capital "T") that animates the subjects of this report. While thinkers like Aldous Huxley popularized a form of perennialism based on shared mystical experiences that could coexist with liberal values, the Traditionalism of René Guénon and Julius Evola is fundamentally different. For them, the Perennial Philosophy is not merely a tool for interfaith understanding but the basis for a totalizing critique and rejection of the modern world itself.
The Traditionalist School: René Guénon's Critique of Modernity
The foundational architect of modern Traditionalism is the French metaphysician René Guénon (1886-1951). His work provides the intellectual diagnosis for what he termed The Crisis of the Modern World.
The "Reign of Quantity" and the Spiritual Crisis
Guénon’s central thesis is that the modern West is in a state of profound spiritual and intellectual decline because it has abandoned traditional metaphysical principles in favor of materialism and rationalism. Modernity, for Guénon, represents the "Reign of Quantity," a civilizational stage where quality, spirit, and transcendent truth have been subordinated to the measurable, the material, and the profitable. He argued that this obsession with the quantitative has led to a "veritable disintegration of human activity," characterized by "ceaseless agitation, for unending change, and for ever-increasing speed". In such a world, humanity becomes a slave to matter, "dooming themselves to ever increasing agitation without rule or objective".
This crisis, Guénon maintained, is "fundamentally a spiritual crisis". By severing its connection to the sacred, the modern world has forgotten the "essential principles that govern existence". The "false values of individualism have led to a fragmentation of society and a detachment from traditional spiritual truths". Without the guidance of a higher, sacred authority, society descends into moral relativism and institutional decay.
The Kali Yuga: A Cyclical View of History
To explain this decline, Guénon adopted the Hindu cosmological concept of cyclical history, which divides time into four ages, or yugas. He argued that the modern era corresponds to the Kali Yuga—the final, "dark age" of conflict, sin, and spiritual degradation that precedes the end of the current cycle. This Hindu eschatology provided a powerful metaphysical framework for Traditionalists. It reframed the narrative of modernity not as one of linear progress, as championed by the Enlightenment, but as one of inevitable decay and a fall from a primordial Golden Age (Satya Yuga). According to this view, the present age is characterized by a deterioration of dharma (moral and cosmic order), where truthfulness, mercy, and physical strength diminish with each passing day.
The Role of the Spiritual Elite
For Guénon, the path back from the abyss of the Kali Yuga was not through mass movements or democratic politics, which he saw as symptoms of the disease. The "opinion of the majority," he wrote, "cannot be anything but an expression of incompetence". Instead, he believed that true metaphysical knowledge is preserved and transmitted through an "initiatic chain" by a spiritual elite. A good society, a "traditional" society, is one that is hierarchical and proceeds directly from a metaphysical truth, with all its institutions oriented toward helping its citizens realize that truth. He drew a sharp distinction between the "sacerdotal" (priestly or sacred) power, which holds the ultimate authority of metaphysical principle, and the "royal" (governmental) power, which should be subordinate to it. The modern deviation, in his view, began with the usurpation of sacred authority by temporal powers, a process he traces back to the destruction of the Knights Templar in 1314. Guénon's vision was for a restoration of this sacred hierarchy, led by an initiated elite capable of reconnecting the profane world with its transcendent source.
The Path of Action: Julius Evola's Heroic Imperative
While Guénon provided the metaphysical diagnosis of modernity's ills, it was the Italian baron Julius Evola (1898-1974) who provided the political and martial prescription. Evola took Guénon's ideas and transformed them from a basis for spiritual contemplation into a call for a radical, aristocratic Revolt Against the Modern World. He rejected Guénon's tendency toward apolitical quietism, arguing instead for a "path of action" to actively combat the degenerative forces of the Kali Yuga. This crucial shift from diagnosis to prescription is what has made Traditionalism so potent as a political ideology. Guénon's work offers a totalizing critique of the liberal order, framing it as a spiritual catastrophe, while Evola's work provides a framework for its violent overthrow, elevating political struggle to the level of a spiritual duty. This dialectic allows adherents to simultaneously embrace esoteric metaphysics and militant activism, a combination seen in figures like Aleksandr Dugin and Steve Bannon.
The "True State" and the Rejection of Modern Politics
Evola's political theory is one of the most consistently "antiegalitarian, antiliberal, antidemocratic, and antipopular systems in the twentieth century". His ideal was a "true state" that is organic, hierarchical, and ruled by a spiritual elite embodying transcendent authority. This state would be anti-democratic, rejecting the mediocrity of majority rule; anti-socialist, rejecting the leveling impulse of collectivism; and anti-capitalist, rejecting the materialism that reduces all values to economic ones. Evola was deeply critical of both Italian Fascism and German Nazism, not from the left, but from the right. He found them to be insufficiently traditionalist, too populist, too materialistic, and still beholden to the modern concept of the nation-state rather than the transcendent ideal of Empire.
The Heroic Ideal and the Metaphysics of War
Central to Evola's thought is the valorization of the "heroic" ideal. He believed that war and violent struggle could serve as a path to spiritual realization, offering a "transfiguring knowledge of life, life according to death". For Evola, the "moment the individual succeeds in living as a hero, even if it is the final moment of his earthly life, weighs infinitely more on the scale of values than a protracted existence spent consuming monotonously among the trivialities of cities".
He drew on the Islamic concept of the two holy wars, arguing that the "lesser holy war" (the external, material battle) could become a vehicle for the "greater holy war" (the internal, spiritual struggle against one's own lower nature). This synthesis of action and contemplation provided a metaphysical justification for political violence, transforming it from a mere worldly necessity into a sacred rite. This "heroic vision of life" was, for Evola, the only force capable of defending "the West" against the "barbaric and elemental forces" unleashed by modernity.
The philosophy of Traditionalism thus functions as a "meta-political" ideology. It does not prescribe specific economic or social policies but rather offers a total worldview that redefines the purpose of the state and society as the restoration of a sacred, hierarchical order. This abstract, metaphysical foundation makes it highly adaptable. Its core anti-liberal tenets can be filled with the specific cultural content of any given civilization—be it Russian Orthodoxy, Hindutva, or a romanticized "Judeo-Christian West"—while retaining a consistent underlying structure. This flexibility allows for the creation of a globally coherent movement that is not culturally homogenous, making it a uniquely resilient ideological challenge to the universalist claims of liberal democracy.
Part II: The Western Vanguard – Weaponizing Tradition in the United States
The esoteric and deeply European philosophy of Traditionalism has found fertile ground in the United States, where it has been adapted and operationalized by key political actors. This section analyzes two distinct but complementary networks that form the American vanguard of the Traditionalist International: Steve Bannon's, which applies Traditionalist concepts to populist mobilization and geopolitical strategy, and Leonard Leo's, which focuses on the systematic, long-term capture of state institutions.
The Bannon Network: Traditionalism as Geopolitical Strategy
Steve Bannon, former White House Chief Strategist and media executive, serves as a primary conduit for translating the arcane metaphysics of Traditionalism into a potent political narrative for a mass audience.
From Metaphysics to "Civilizational Clash"
Bannon operationalizes the Traditionalist concept of the Kali Yuga through more accessible, secular frameworks. He is a noted proponent of the generational theory of William Strauss and Neil Howe, particularly their book The Fourth Turning, which posits that American history moves in 80-year cycles culminating in an era of catastrophic crisis and violent renewal. This theory, which dabbles in the same cyclical temporality as Traditionalism, provides Bannon with a historical narrative of inevitable decline and collapse, mirroring the Kali Yuga. He has also explicitly referenced the Hindu concept in interviews, demonstrating his familiarity with its esoteric roots.
This framework of crisis allows Bannon to reframe contemporary geopolitics as an apocalyptic "clash of civilizations". He portrays the world as locked in an existential struggle between a decadent but salvageable "Judeo-Christian West" and its two primary civilizational adversaries: "jihadist Islamic fascism" and a predatory, expansionist China. This rhetoric transforms political and economic competition into a spiritual war, a core tenet of the Evolian worldview.
The Dugin-Bannon Nexus
Bannon’s intellectual kinship with key Traditionalist figures is well-established. He has openly acknowledged the influence of Julius Evola and has expressed admiration for the Russian philosopher Aleksandr Dugin, calling him "one of the greatest, most influential geopolitical thinkers". The ideological alignment and strategic divergence between these two figures were starkly illustrated during a secret eight-hour meeting in Rome in November 2018.
During their discussion, both men found common ground in their total rejection of the liberal order, agreeing that modernity represents "pure nothingness". However, they clashed on two fundamental points. First, Dugin, as a consistent Traditionalist, views the United States as the very epitome of the modern, anti-traditional world—the heart of the Kali Yuga. Bannon, an American nationalist, counters this by positing a Gnostic-like distinction between a "true," primordial America rooted in Judeo-Christian values and a corrupt "globalist" America that has usurped it. This ideological maneuver allows Bannon to weaponize an anti-modern philosophy in the service of a nationalist project, resolving a core contradiction for its application in an American context. Second, they disagreed on the primary enemy: Bannon identified China as the ultimate threat, urging a Russian-American axis against it, whereas Dugin's entire geopolitical framework is built on a Eurasian bloc united against the "Atlanticist" United States.
Building the International Network
Following his departure from the White House, Bannon declared his intention to build "the infrastructure, globally, for the global populist movement". This ambition took concrete form in "The Movement," a Brussels-based foundation launched in 2018 with the goal of uniting Europe's right-wing populist and nationalist parties into a "supergroup" within the European Parliament. Bannon envisioned his organization as a counterforce to George Soros's Open Society Foundations, providing polling, data analytics, and strategic advice to parties seeking to dismantle the European Union—an institution he views as a primary agent of detested "globalism".
While "The Movement" attracted initial interest from figures like Italy's Matteo Salvini and Hungary's Viktor Orbán, it ultimately stalled. The project was undermined by the inherent contradictions of a nationalist international—many parties were wary of perceived American influence—and by complex European election laws that restrict foreign funding and in-kind donations. Despite its operational failure, the effort clearly demonstrated Bannon's strategic intent to construct a formal transatlantic network based on shared anti-liberal, anti-globalist, and traditionalist values.
The Leo Network: Reshaping American Institutions from Within
While Bannon focuses on populist mobilization and external disruption, conservative legal activist Leonard Leo has pursued a quieter, more methodical strategy of internal capture, focusing on the long-term transformation of American state institutions. This dual approach makes the American Traditionalist movement uniquely formidable: Bannon's populism generates the political chaos necessary for anti-establishment figures to gain power, while Leo's network provides the institutional tools and personnel to consolidate that power and enact a permanent shift in the nation's legal and cultural foundations.
The Architect of Judicial Conquest
For over three decades, Leonard Leo has been the architect of the conservative legal movement's conquest of the American judiciary, primarily through his role as the longtime executive vice president and current co-chairman of the Federalist Society. This project aligns with Traditionalist principles by seeking to replace the perceived moral relativism of modern liberal jurisprudence with a return to a fixed, authoritative interpretation of law. The doctrines of originalism and textualism, which hold that the Constitution's meaning is fixed at the time of its enactment, function as a legal analog to the Traditionalist concept of a sacred, unchanging truth.
Leo's strategy has been to create a pipeline for a new legal elite, identifying and cultivating conservative law students and guiding them into positions of power. He famously compiled the list of potential Supreme Court nominees from which President Donald Trump selected Neil Gorsuch, Brett Kavanaugh, and Amy Coney Barrett, cementing a conservative supermajority on the nation's highest court. This decades-long effort has successfully installed an elite committed to a hierarchical and ordered vision of society, resulting in the overturning of landmark precedents on abortion rights, the expansion of gun rights, and the restriction of environmental regulations.
The Dark Money Machine
Leo's influence is underwritten by one of the most sophisticated and opaque funding networks in American politics. This network of interlocking non-profits allows him to channel vast sums of "dark money" from anonymous donors into his political and cultural projects. Key organizations in this web include:
* Marble Freedom Trust: In 2022, this Leo-helmed group received a staggering $1.6 billion donation from businessman Barre Seid, believed to be the largest single contribution to a political advocacy group in U.S. history. This fund provides a virtually inexhaustible war chest for Leo's future endeavors.
* The 85 Fund: Formerly known as the Judicial Education Project, this 501(c)(3) organization serves as a central funding hub, distributing grants to a wide range of conservative groups. It also operates under the alias Honest Elections Project, which litigates and runs ad campaigns to promote restrictive voting laws.
* Concord Fund: Formerly the Judicial Crisis Network, this 501(c)(4) group is the public-facing attack dog of the network. It spends tens of millions of dollars on aggressive media campaigns to support the confirmation of conservative judicial nominees and to oppose those deemed ideologically unsuitable.
This financial apparatus provides the permanent infrastructure for a long-term political and cultural counter-revolution, insulating it from the vagaries of election cycles and public opinion.
From Legal Theory to Christian Nationalism
Leo's ambitions now extend far beyond the judiciary. He has stated his goal is to "roll back liberal dominance in other areas of American cultural, policy and political life" and to wage a broader culture war against "wokeism" and the "vile and immoral current-day barbarians, secularists and bigots" who threaten society's "natural order".
His network is a prime financial engine of the Christian Nationalist movement, which seeks to dismantle the separation of church and state and codify a specific, conservative Christian worldview into law. Leo-affiliated groups are key partners in initiatives like Project 2025, a comprehensive plan to staff a future conservative administration with loyalists and implement a sweeping agenda that includes banning abortion, rolling back LGBTQ+ rights, and promoting a "biblically based" definition of marriage and family. This agenda represents a direct attempt to translate the Traditionalist call for a return to a sacred, hierarchical social order into concrete American policy.
Part III: The Eurasian Axis – State-Sponsored Civilizational Alternatives
While Traditionalist ideas have influenced non-state actors and political movements in the West, in Russia and China they have been absorbed and repurposed by state powers to construct official ideologies. These ideologies serve to legitimize authoritarian rule, create a national identity in opposition to the West, and articulate a vision for an alternative international order. However, the methods of adoption differ significantly: Russia has undertaken a deep ideological synthesis, while China has pursued a more pragmatic and instrumental co-optation of traditional concepts.
The Putin Model: Orthodox Traditionalism as State Philosophy
Under the leadership of Vladimir Putin, the Russian state has systematically cultivated an official ideology that fuses Russian Orthodox mysticism, Eurasianist geopolitics, and a defense of "traditional values" in direct opposition to Western liberalism.
"Moscow, The Third Rome" and "Traditional Values"
The Kremlin's ideology revives the historical-messianic concept of "Moscow, the Third Rome," which posits Russia as the true successor to the Roman and Byzantine Empires and the last bastion of authentic Christianity. This narrative frames Russia not as a peripheral European nation but as a unique "state-civilization" with a sacred mission to defend traditional morality against a "decadent" and "satanic" West. This worldview is formally enshrined in state policy. A 2022 presidential decree, "On the Approval of the Foundations of State Policy for the Preservation and Strengthening of Traditional Russian Spiritual and Moral Values," defines these values as including patriotism, a strong family, and "the priority of the spiritual over the material"—a direct echo of Guénon's critique of modernity. These ideas are now being systematically integrated into the national education system to shape future generations.
The Dugin Influence: The Fourth Political Theory
The key intellectual architect of this synthesis is Aleksandr Dugin, a philosopher who has spent decades adapting the European Traditionalism of Guénon and Evola to a Russian context. Dugin's work combines Traditionalist metaphysics with a geopolitical theory known as Eurasianism, which views world history as an eternal struggle between land-based, traditionalist civilizations (Eurasia, led by Russia) and sea-based, materialistic, liberal civilizations ("Atlantis," led by the United States).
His most notable contribution is the "Fourth Political Theory," an explicit attempt to create a new political ideology to supersede liberalism, communism, and fascism. It rejects the core subjects of the first three theories—the individual, class, and the nation-state/race, respectively—and proposes Dasein (a concept borrowed from Martin Heidegger, meaning "being-there" or existence) as the new subject. For Dugin, this means that each civilization, or ethnos, has its own unique way of being and should be the basis of political organization. The ultimate goal is a multipolar world of distinct civilizational blocs, free from the universalist pretensions of Western liberalism.
From Ideology to War
This Traditionalist-Eurasianist framework provides the direct ideological justification for Russia's aggressive foreign policy. The concept of the Russkiy Mir ("Russian World")—a spiritual and cultural space encompassing all Eastern Slavs—was used to legitimize the 2014 annexation of Crimea and the 2022 full-scale invasion of Ukraine. The war is framed not as a conflict between two states, but as a civilizational and metaphysical struggle to protect "Holy Rus" from a West that seeks to impose "perversions" like "gay pride parades".
The Russian Orthodox Church (ROC), under the leadership of Patriarch Kirill, has become a crucial partner in this project. Kirill has described Putin's rule as a "miracle of God" and has provided theological justification for the war, calling it a "Holy War" against the "forces of evil" and promising soldiers that dying in Ukraine "washes away all sins". This deep alignment between church and state has transformed the ROC into an ideological extension of the Kremlin, spreading its propaganda both domestically and abroad. While Dugin's direct influence on Putin is debated, his ideas have undeniably permeated the thinking of Russia's military, police, and statist foreign policy elites, with his 1997 book Foundations of Geopolitics being highly influential.
The Xi Jinping Model: Confucian Socialism and the Sinicization of Tradition
The Chinese Communist Party (CCP) under Xi Jinping has also turned to tradition to bolster its rule, but its approach is one of pragmatic and selective instrumentalization rather than deep ideological synthesis.
Co-opting Tradition for Legitimacy
In a marked reversal of the iconoclasm of the Mao Zedong era, which aggressively sought to destroy the "Four Olds" (old ideas, culture, customs, and habits), the CCP now promotes what it calls "excellent traditional culture" as a source of national pride and political legitimacy. As the ideological appeal of Marxism-Leninism has waned, the Party has selectively revived Confucian concepts such as harmony (Hexie), social order, hierarchy, and deference to authority. This allows the CCP to present its authoritarian, one-party system not as a foreign communist import, but as a modern expression of China's unique civilizational heritage, thereby deflecting Western critiques of its lack of democracy. The Party, in this narrative, positions itself as the sole legitimate inheritor and protector of 5,000 years of Chinese civilization.
"A Community of Common Destiny"
China's vision for an alternative international order is encapsulated in Xi Jinping's signature phrase, "a community of common destiny for mankind" (now officially translated as "a community of shared future for mankind"). This concept challenges the liberal international order by advocating for a multipolar world based on the principles of sovereign equality, mutual non-interference, and respect for civilizational diversity. It implicitly rejects the universality of Western liberal values, such as individual human rights and multi-party democracy, and instead offers "socialism with Chinese characteristics" as a "new option" for developing nations seeking to modernize while preserving their political independence. This vision is being operationalized through massive infrastructure and investment projects like the Belt and Road Initiative, which serves as the practical platform for building this new Sinocentric order.
The Russia-China "Friendship"
The strategic alignment between Russia and China is best understood as a pragmatic "political friendship" built on a shared grievance against the US-led global order, rather than a formal, ideologically-bound alliance. While they coordinate closely in international forums like the United Nations Security Council, their long-term interests and visions for global governance are not fully aligned.
A key strategy of this axis is not to reject the normative language of the liberal order, but to co-opt and redefine it. A 2022 joint statement by Putin and Xi provides a clear example, championing "such universal human values as peace, development, equality, justice, democracy and freedom". However, they strip these terms of their liberal content, defining democracy as simply a means for "citizens' participation in the government of their country" that respects national traditions—a definition that comfortably accommodates their authoritarian systems. They simultaneously condemn the "abuse of democratic values and interference in the internal affairs of sovereign states under the pretext of protecting democracy and human rights," effectively neutralizing the West's ability to criticize their domestic repression. This sophisticated ideological maneuver allows them to present themselves as defenders of "genuine democracy" while actively undermining its liberal foundations.
Part IV: Global South Manifestations – Adapting the Anti-Modern Revolt
The Traditionalist critique of modernity finds a powerful resonance in post-colonial contexts, where the "decadence" of the West is often conflated with the legacy of colonialism and the imposition of foreign values. In India and Turkey, leaders have skillfully adapted Traditionalist themes, framing their authoritarian and majoritarian projects as acts of decolonization and civilizational self-assertion. They map the narrative of the Kali Yuga onto their national histories, identifying a past "Golden Age" that was corrupted by foreign domination (Islamic and British rule in India, Westernization in Turkey) and casting their political movements as the vehicle for a national and spiritual restoration.
Narendra Modi's India: Hindutva as National Tradition
Under Prime Minister Narendra Modi and the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), the political ideology of Hindutva has moved from the fringes to the center of Indian political life, fundamentally reshaping the nation's identity.
From Hinduism to Hindutva
It is essential to distinguish Hindutva ("Hindu-ness") from the vast and diverse religious tradition of Hinduism. Hindutva is a modern political ideology, first articulated by Vinayak Damodar Savarkar in the 1920s. It defines Indian identity not strictly by religious practice—Savarkar himself was an atheist—but by a shared culture, race, and sacred geography, with India as the Pitribhumi (Fatherland) and Punyabhumi (Holyland). This ethno-nationalist construction includes Sikhs, Buddhists, and Jains as part of the Hindu civilizational family but implicitly excludes Muslims and Christians, whose holy lands are outside India, as foreign elements.
Rewriting the Nation as a Hindu Rashtra
The central political project of the Hindutva movement is to transform India from the secular republic envisioned by its founders into a Hindu Rashtra (Hindu Nation). This requires a radical rewriting of Indian history, which is a core activity of Hindutva ideologues. They promote a narrative of a glorious, ancient Hindu golden age characterized by scientific and spiritual supremacy, which was interrupted and suppressed by "a thousand years of slavery" under successive Muslim and British rulers. This story of decline and the need for restoration is a direct parallel to the Traditionalist conception of history as a descent into the Kali Yuga, with modernity and secularism representing the final stages of degradation.
Policy and Impact on Minorities
Under Modi's premiership, this ideology has been translated into concrete state policy, with severe consequences for India's religious minorities, particularly its 200 million Muslims. The 2019 Citizenship Amendment Act (CAA), for example, creates a religion-based pathway to citizenship for refugees from neighboring countries, specifically including Hindus, Sikhs, Buddhists, Jains, Parsis, and Christians, while pointedly excluding Muslims. This act, for the first time in India's history, introduces a religious test for citizenship, undermining the secular principles of the constitution and violating international human rights obligations.
When combined with a proposed National Register of Citizens (NRC), critics fear the CAA could be used to strip millions of Indian Muslims of their citizenship. Furthermore, state-level "anti-conversion" laws, promoted by Hindutva groups, are used to restrict religious freedom and target minorities. This legislative push has been accompanied by a rise in vigilante violence. "Cow protection" mobs have lynched Muslims and Dalits accused of trading or consuming beef, often with the tacit approval or inaction of local authorities, creating a climate of fear and institutionalizing a form of second-class citizenship akin to the Jim Crow era in the United States.
Ideological Parallels and Alliances
The Hindutva movement's ideological roots show a clear affinity with European far-right thought. Early Hindutva thinkers like Savarkar and M.S. Golwalkar were inspired by the fascist movements in Italy and Germany, with Golwalkar explicitly praising Nazi Germany's "purging" of the Jews as "a good lesson for us in Hindusthan to learn and profit by". This historical connection has been revived in the contemporary era. Today, Hindu nationalists are forging alliances with the European New Right and other global far-right movements, based on a shared platform of Islamophobia, anti-immigrant sentiment, and civilizational nationalism. Figures like the Nazi-sympathizing occultist Savitri Devi, who synthesized Nazism with a distorted vision of Hinduism and was an influential figure in post-war neo-Nazism, serve as a historical and ideological bridge between these seemingly disparate movements, influencing both the Hindutva movement and the modern alt-right.
Recep Tayyip Erdoğan's Turkey: Islamic Conservatism and Neo-Ottomanism
The political trajectory of Turkey under President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan provides another powerful example of a leader leveraging traditionalist and civilizational narratives to consolidate authoritarian rule and project power abroad.
The Rejection of Kemalism
Erdoğan's rise and the dominance of his Justice and Development Party (AKP) represent a systematic dismantling of Kemalism, the secular, Westernizing, and nationalist ideology that has defined the Turkish Republic since its founding by Mustafa Kemal Atatürk. In its place, Erdoğan has promoted a socially conservative, Islamist vision for Turkey, seeking to re-center the nation's identity around its Ottoman and Islamic heritage. This project is a direct rejection of what Traditionalists would see as the "profane" and "anti-traditional" nature of the Kemalist state.
Erdoğanism as a Populist Synthesis
The ideology that has emerged, often termed "Erdoğanism," is a populist synthesis centered on Erdoğan's own charismatic authority. It combines elements of Turkish nationalism, Pan-Islamism, and a populist anti-Western, anti-elitist rhetoric. This mirrors the Traditionalist emphasis on a strong, hierarchical leader who embodies the spiritual and political will of the nation, standing in opposition to corrupt, secular elites. Erdoğan's rhetoric consistently employs us-versus-them binaries, framing his supporters as the "true" nation (yerli ve milli - homegrown and national) and his opponents as foreign agents or traitors.
Neo-Ottomanism and the "Century of Turkey"
Erdoğan's foreign policy is animated by neo-Ottomanism, an irredentist and expansionist ideology that seeks to restore Turkish influence in regions formerly under Ottoman rule, from the Balkans to the Middle East and North Africa. This ambition is framed as a glorious revival of a past golden age, a restoration of Turkey's rightful place in the world after a century of decline under the Kemalist order. This narrative of decline and rebirth is structurally identical to the Traditionalist historical cycle. Erdoğan's "Century of Turkey" vision is a forward-looking articulation of this goal, aiming to establish Turkey as a major civilizational pole in a multipolar world, contributing to a "fairer" international system that is not dominated by the West.
The Diyanet as a Global Tool
A key instrument in this project is the Presidency of Religious Affairs, or Diyanet. Originally established by Atatürk to control religion, the Diyanet has been transformed under Erdoğan into a powerful and well-funded tool of statecraft and global soft power. Its budget has been massively expanded, far exceeding that of many government ministries, and its staff has grown to nearly 150,000. The Diyanet now operates in 145 countries, running thousands of mosques, schools, and cultural centers that promote Erdoğan's specific brand of Turkish Islamic conservatism. It also functions as an intelligence-gathering arm of the state, monitoring the Turkish diaspora and perceived political enemies abroad.
Across these diverse non-Western contexts, a common political form emerges: the "civilization-state." Leaders in Russia, China, India, and Turkey all reject the Western model of a pluralistic, civic nation-state. Instead, they define their nations in civilizational terms, rooted in a single, dominant spiritual or cultural tradition. This redefinition has profound consequences, justifying both a hierarchical domestic order that marginalizes minorities and an expansionist foreign policy aimed at uniting or protecting the members of their civilization beyond the state's borders. The civilization-state is the political architecture of the Traditionalist International.
Part V: The Traditionalist International in Action: A Comparative Synthesis
This section provides a systematic comparison of how Traditionalist principles are operationalized across the different case studies. It will analyze the common strategic playbook employed by these actors: the rewriting of national history to fit a narrative of decline and restoration; the co-optation of mainstream religious institutions to provide spiritual legitimacy; and the framing of all political opposition in civilizational terms as a battle against a corrupt, decadent, and alien "other."
Comparative Framework: Ideology, Strategy, and Contradictions
The convergence of these disparate movements is not accidental; it stems from the application of a shared ideological toolkit to varying local conditions. A common strategic playbook is evident:
1. Historical Revisionism: Each movement constructs a narrative of a glorious, traditional past (a Golden Age) that was lost or corrupted by the forces of modernity—identified variously as Western liberalism, colonialism, secularism, or globalism. The political project is then framed as a necessary and heroic restoration of this lost order.
2. Co-optation of Religious Authority: Mainstream religious institutions are transformed into arms of the state, providing a veneer of sacred legitimacy for the authoritarian project. The Russian Orthodox Church, the Sangh Parivar in India, and Turkey's Diyanet are all examples of religious bodies being leveraged to sanctify political power and mobilize the populace.
3. Civilizational Othering: Political conflict is redefined as a civilizational struggle. Domestic opponents are cast not as legitimate political actors but as traitors and agents of a hostile foreign civilization. International relations are viewed through the lens of a clash between a virtuous, traditional "us" and a corrupt, decadent "them."
Despite these commonalities, the Traditionalist International is fraught with internal contradictions and potential fault lines. The inherent nationalism of each movement creates rivalries. For instance, Steve Bannon's fervent Sinophobia directly clashes with Aleksandr Dugin's vision of a Eurasian bloc in which China is a key partner. Similarly, the competing claims of Russia, China, Turkey, and India to be the true leaders of a new, non-Western traditionalist order create a basis for future conflict rather than stable alliance.

	Part VI: Consequences and Countervailing Forces
The rise of the Traditionalist International has profound and tangible consequences for human rights, international law, and the stability of the liberal world order. This final section assesses these real-world impacts and provides a brief overview of the forces resisting this global authoritarian convergence.
The Impact on Human Rights and International Law
The Traditionalist worldview, with its emphasis on hierarchy, collective identity, and a "natural order," is fundamentally at odds with the modern human rights framework, which is rooted in the principles of individual autonomy, equality, and universalism.
Gender, Sexuality, and the "Traditional Family"
A primary target of Traditionalist-inspired governance is the rollback of rights for women and LGBTQ+ individuals. These groups are often portrayed as symbols of modern Western decadence and a threat to the "traditional family," which is upheld as the sacred foundation of the nation. In Russia, this has manifested in the infamous 2013 "gay propaganda" law, which effectively criminalizes any public expression of non-traditional sexual relationships, and a subsequent 2023 Supreme Court ruling that banned the non-existent "international LGBT movement" as an "extremist organization," leading to widespread persecution. In Turkey, President Erdoğan justified his country's withdrawal from the Istanbul Convention, a landmark treaty on preventing violence against women, with the claim that it "normalizes homosexuality". In the United States, the Christian Nationalist movement, heavily funded by Leonard Leo's network, is at the forefront of the campaign to ban abortion and restrict LGBTQ+ rights, framing these efforts as a necessary restoration of "biblically based" family values.
Religious Minorities and Ethno-Nationalism
The Traditionalist emphasis on a homogenous civilizational identity inevitably leads to the marginalization and persecution of religious and ethnic minorities who do not fit the prescribed mold. In India, the Hindutva project's definition of the nation as fundamentally Hindu has fueled discrimination and violence against the country's large Muslim population. Policies like the Citizenship Amendment Act (CAA) and the rise of state-condoned vigilante violence have created a system of second-class citizenship, threatening the secular fabric of Indian democracy. Russian state propaganda has also employed anti-Semitic tropes, blaming Jewish people, including Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, for the war in Ukraine and framing the conflict as a defense of Christian civilization.
Suppression of Dissent and Civil Society
To maintain their hierarchical order, these regimes systematically suppress dissent and dismantle civil society, which are seen as conduits for corrosive Western influence. China's social credit system represents a technologically advanced model of this, creating a vast surveillance apparatus to enforce ideological conformity and punish undesirable behavior. In Russia, laws targeting "foreign agents" and "extremism" are used to silence human rights organizations, independent media, and any form of political opposition. Similarly, in Turkey, the government has used broadly defined anti-terrorism laws to jail journalists, academics, and activists, hollowing out democratic institutions.
The Challenge to the Liberal International Order
The Traditionalist International poses a multifaceted and fundamental challenge to the post-World War II liberal international order. It is not merely a geopolitical challenge from revisionist states but an ideological one that strikes at the normative foundations of the system. By rejecting the universality of liberal values, promoting a multipolar world of competing civilizational blocs, and actively working to undermine international institutions and human rights law, these actors seek to replace the current order with one more amenable to their authoritarian models.
A particularly insidious aspect of this challenge is the way in which these regimes co-opt the language of liberal resistance. When Western governments or human rights organizations criticize domestic repression, this criticism is not engaged with on its merits but is instead framed as an act of cultural imperialism or geopolitical aggression. This creates a self-reinforcing ideological loop: domestic repression is justified as a necessary defense against foreign interference, and the inevitable foreign criticism of that repression is then presented as proof of the original threat. This paradox makes the Traditionalist narrative highly resilient to external pressure and effectively turns liberal resistance into fuel for the authoritarian engine.
Forms of Resistance
Despite the formidable nature of this global convergence, it is not without opposition. Countervailing forces are active both within these societies and on the international stage.
* Pro-Democracy Movements: Civil society continues to resist. In India, the passage of the CAA sparked massive, nationwide pro-democracy protests led by a diverse coalition of students, women, and civil society groups. In Turkey, despite severe repression, opposition movements and protests against the erosion of democratic rights persist.
* Dissenting Religious Voices: Resistance also emerges from within the very religious traditions that these regimes claim to champion. In Russia, nearly 300 Orthodox priests and deacons signed an open letter condemning the invasion of Ukraine, a significant act of dissent against Patriarch Kirill's pro-war stance. In the United States, a growing number of Christian groups, such as Christians Against Christian Nationalism, are actively organizing to oppose the conflation of their faith with a nationalist political ideology.
* Liberal Counter-Offensives: International human rights organizations like Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch continue to play a crucial role in documenting abuses and advocating for victims. Liberal democratic states, while often inconsistent, provide support for civil society and apply diplomatic and economic pressure on these regimes.
Conclusion: The Perennial Resurgence of Authoritarianism
Summary of Findings
The analysis presented in this report demonstrates that the contemporary global rise of authoritarianism is not a series of isolated phenomena but a convergent trend animated by a shared ideological engine: the philosophy of Traditionalism. Originating with the metaphysical critique of modernity by René Guénon and radicalized into a political call to arms by Julius Evola, Traditionalism provides a powerful and adaptable toolkit for 21st-century authoritarians. Its core tenets—the rejection of liberal materialism, a belief in cyclical history and the current "dark age" of the Kali Yuga, and a call for the restoration of sacred hierarchy—offer a profound and resonant justification for dismantling democratic institutions.
From the geopolitical strategies of Steve Bannon and the institutional capture orchestrated by Leonard Leo in the United States, to the state-sponsored Orthodox Traditionalism of Vladimir Putin's Russia, the pragmatic co-optation of Confucian values by Xi Jinping's China, the Hindutva project of Narendra Modi's India, and the neo-Ottoman vision of Recep Tayyip Erdoğan's Turkey, a clear pattern emerges. These leaders and movements, while culturally distinct, are united in a common project: to replace the liberal, individualistic, and secular order with one that is hierarchical, collectivist, and rooted in a redefined, state-sanctioned "tradition."
Future Outlook
The long-term viability of this "Traditionalist International" remains an open question. Its strength lies in its ideological flexibility and its shared opposition to the liberal order. However, its profound internal contradictions—particularly the clash between the universalist claims of its underlying philosophy and the chauvinistic nationalisms of its political manifestations—may ultimately limit its ability to form a stable and coherent alternative world order. The rivalry between Bannon's America-first vision and Dugin's Eurasianism, and the competing civilizational claims of Russia, China, India, and Turkey, suggest a future of conflict rather than cooperation.
Nevertheless, the appeal of the Traditionalist narrative as a response to the perceived spiritual emptiness, social fragmentation, and economic precarity of late-modern globalization should not be underestimated. As long as liberal democracies struggle to address these deep-seated anxieties, the perennial philosophy will continue to fuel the perennial resurgence of authoritarianism. The ability of liberal democracy to mount an effective response will depend not only on its geopolitical and economic strength but on its capacity to offer a compelling vision of meaning, community, and purpose that can withstand this spiritually framed challenge.
Key Recommendations for Further Study
To deepen the understanding of this critical global trend, further research is recommended in the following areas:
* The Role of Religious Institutions: A more granular analysis is needed of how specific religious institutions, both mainstream and fringe, are engaged in either promoting or resisting the spread of Traditionalist ideas within their communities and across borders. This includes examining funding flows, theological debates, and the role of diasporic communities.
* Comparative Analysis of Non-Western Contexts: While this report has focused on key case studies, the influence of Traditionalist and similar anti-modern ideologies is growing in other regions, including Latin America and Africa. Comparative analysis of these movements would provide a more comprehensive picture of the phenomenon's global scope and its various adaptations.
* The Impact on Human Rights and International Law: Further research should focus on the specific legal and institutional mechanisms by which the Traditionalist agenda is eroding the foundations of international human rights law. This includes analyzing the rhetoric used in international forums like the UN, the development of alternative legal norms based on "traditional values," and the potential for new legal and diplomatic strategies to defend the universalist human rights framework.
